Why this forecast

Anchor: loglinear (damped exponential) had the lowest backtest MAE at every horizon (h3 MAE 1503 vs 3344-4641 for the others) and its bias-corrected medians (21553/30291/37320/52159) sit at the top of the method spread, consistent with the recent trajectory: admissions have risen 13 consecutive weeks, +13% wk/wk last week after +19% and +16%, and ILI is still climbing (3.80% and rising). I trimmed the loglinear medians slightly (20000/25000/30000/38000) because growth is decelerating (13% < 19% < 16% pattern suggests the peak is near) and the last observed week (13668) is provisional and likely to revise upward, so h0 should sit above it. The hub ensemble had not submitted, so no external prior to blend; the flat baseline (13668) is clearly too low given the trend. Intervals: I used lognormal shapes with sigma widening by horizon (0.16/0.21/0.23/0.25), wider than the backtest-implied intervals because the backtest assumes the recent past repeats, and this is a fast-rising season approaching a possible peak/inflection where flu can turn faster than it looks; the right tail is longer than the left to reflect right-skew and the risk of a sharper surge. What would change my mind: if next week's observed count is below ~17000, the peak is likely already here and I would shift mass down; if it exceeds ~25000, the season is accelerating and I would shift up.

Anchor: the statistical-model consensus (median across methods) from compare_forecast_models, which sits at 16.9k/21.2k/25.6k/31.6k for h0-h3. I trimmed the h0-h2 medians slightly toward the backtest-best methods (seasonal MAE 1349, gbt 2469, loglinear 1630) to 16.5k/20.0k/23.5k, and set h3 at 27.0k, below the seasonal analogue's 41k because last year's peak (42.5k in early Jan) came ~6 weeks after this point with growth still accelerating, whereas this season's growth is decelerating (+33% -> +16% -> +14% wk/wk) and the season started earlier (mid-October high activity, 7 states at high ILI by Nov 7), implying the peak arrives sooner and the h3 week is closer to it. The hub ensemble had no submission for this week, so I had no ensemble prior to lean on; the flat baseline (11.5k) is clearly wrong given the rising trajectory. Evidence for the upward tilt vs persistence: ILI at 3.23% and still rising, 11.3% of lab tests positive, CDC reporting an early season with 36k hospitalizations already, and last year from this exact point admissions went 11.5k -> 21k -> 37.5k -> 42.5k. I keep the medians below the seasonal analogue because the decelerating growth rate and earlier start argue for a flatter, earlier peak. Intervals are lognormal, right-skewed, and widened with horizon (sigma 0.16 -> 0.24) to cover the method disagreement (1.2-1.5x spread in medians), the upward revision risk in the provisional last week, and the possibility of a faster surge as Thanksgiving gatherings drive transmission. What would change my mind: if next week's actual comes in below ~15k (growth stalling), the whole trajectory is lower and the peak is modest; if it exceeds ~20k, the seasonal-analogue path is back in play and h2-h3 should be raised toward 30k-40k.

How to read this

WIS (the weighted interval score) is what FluSight ranks on. It rewards intervals that are narrow and punishes ones that miss, and for a forecast with no uncertainty it is just the absolute error — so it compares models that express very different amounts of doubt. Lower is better.

Relative WIS divides that by the flat baseline's score for the same weeks. The absolute number mostly measures the week: a quiet week is easy for everyone and a turning point is hard for everyone. The ratio is what transfers.

Rank is against every team that submitted to the hub for the same reference date and covered every horizon with published truth. A team that covered half the horizons is answering an easier question and is left out rather than allowed to win on it.

Coverage is how often the truth fell inside the stated interval. A well calibrated 95% interval contains the answer 95% of the time; much more than that means the forecast is hedging.

Forecasts are made by a locally served Qwen with tools, and scored with the weighted interval score against the FluSight hub's published truth. Everything here is generated from the CSVs in forecasts/ by flufc site.
