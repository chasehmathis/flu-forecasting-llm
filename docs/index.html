Why this forecast

Anchor: backtest-weighted statistical models. GBT had the lowest h3 MAE (218) and seasonal the lowest h1/h2; I blended their medians (h1 2450, h2 3050, h3 3900), pulling h3 down from GBT's 5303 because the 2024-25 analogue (a mild season with a low September base) suggests the current early rise is more like a normal-season start than a severe one. No hub ensemble exists for this week (off-season), so no external prior to beat. Adjustments: ILINet %ILI is rising (0.87→1.58 over 5 weeks) and admissions have grown ~25%/wk for 4 weeks, confirming a genuine early-season start rather than noise, so I kept the upward trajectory rather than flattening it. The last observed week (1964) is provisional and likely to revise upward, so h0 median sits slightly above it (2000) with a wide interval. Intervals widen with horizon and are right-skewed (lognormal with larger upper sigma) to cover the possibility of a faster takeoff: h3 spans 1688-11108, covering both a stall and a 2023-24-style early surge. What would change my mind: a flattening of %ILI or admissions next week would argue for a lower h2/h3; a jump to >3000 in the next observed week would argue for moving toward the GBT upper tail.

How to read this

WIS (the weighted interval score) is what FluSight ranks on. It rewards intervals that are narrow and punishes ones that miss, and for a forecast with no uncertainty it is just the absolute error — so it compares models that express very different amounts of doubt. Lower is better.

Relative WIS divides that by the flat baseline's score for the same weeks. The absolute number mostly measures the week: a quiet week is easy for everyone and a turning point is hard for everyone. The ratio is what transfers.

Rank is against every team that submitted to the hub for the same reference date and covered every horizon with published truth. A team that covered half the horizons is answering an easier question and is left out rather than allowed to win on it.

Coverage is how often the truth fell inside the stated interval. A well calibrated 95% interval contains the answer 95% of the time; much more than that means the forecast is hedging.

Forecasts are made by a locally served Qwen with tools, and scored with the weighted interval score against the FluSight hub's published truth. Everything here is generated from the CSVs in forecasts/ by flufc site.
