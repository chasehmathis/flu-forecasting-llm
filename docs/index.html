Why this forecast

Anchor: statistical models, weighted by backtest error. GBT had the lowest backtest MAE at h3 (89.6) and its h0 median (796) matches the recent uptick; the seasonal analogue was best at h0 (98.3) and sits higher (892). No FluSight hub ensemble exists for this off-season week, so no ensemble prior. I started from the GBT medians (796/807/937/1189) and moved them up modestly to 830/870/960/1080: admissions have risen two weeks running (620→677→795, +17% last week) and national ILI has turned up from its July floor (0.87→0.94→1.11, +18% wk/wk), consistent with an early 2026-27 season start rather than noise; the provisional last week also tends to revise upward. I did not take the full seasonal-analogue level because the 2025-26 season only began rising in mid-October, so calendar-week analogues overstate near-term growth. Intervals are lognormal, right-skewed, and widened well beyond the model backtest intervals (e.g. h3 700-1710 vs GBT 797-1775) because we are near a possible seasonal inflection where flu can turn faster than it looks, and the backtest intervals assume the near future resembles the recent flat summer. I would revise upward if ILI keeps doubling its weekly growth or if state-level admissions show a sustained multi-week rise; downward if the uptick stalls and ILI reverts to ~0.9.

How to read this

WIS (the weighted interval score) is what FluSight ranks on. It rewards intervals that are narrow and punishes ones that miss, and for a forecast with no uncertainty it is just the absolute error — so it compares models that express very different amounts of doubt. Lower is better.

Relative WIS divides that by the flat baseline's score for the same weeks. The absolute number mostly measures the week: a quiet week is easy for everyone and a turning point is hard for everyone. The ratio is what transfers.

Rank is against every team that submitted to the hub for the same reference date and covered every horizon with published truth. A team that covered half the horizons is answering an easier question and is left out rather than allowed to win on it.

Coverage is how often the truth fell inside the stated interval. A well calibrated 95% interval contains the answer 95% of the time; much more than that means the forecast is hedging.

Forecasts are made by a locally served Qwen with tools, and scored with the weighted interval score against the FluSight hub's published truth. Everything here is generated from the CSVs in forecasts/ by flufc site.
